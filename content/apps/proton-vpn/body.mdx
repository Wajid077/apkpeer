## What is Proton VPN?

Proton VPN is a VPN app from Proton AG, the Swiss company that also makes Proton Mail, Proton Drive and Proton Pass. Like any VPN, it encrypts your phone's traffic and sends it through Proton's servers, hiding your browsing from the network you're on and letting you appear to be in another country.

What makes it stand out among [VPN apps](/category/vpn) is the free plan. Most free VPNs pay for themselves with ads, data caps or by monetizing your traffic. Proton VPN's free plan has no ads and no data limit, and it's paid for by people on the paid plans instead.

Proton also leans on transparency. The Android app is [open source on GitHub](https://github.com/ProtonVPN/android-app), the company is based in Switzerland, and its no-logs policy has been checked in independent audits. It's been on Google Play since December 2019 and has more than 100 million downloads.

## Key features

### A free plan with no ads or data cap

The free plan connects you to servers in a handful of countries with no ads, no data limit and no time limit. The catch: one device at a time, and Proton picks the server for you. It's enough for protecting yourself on public Wi-Fi every day.

### Secure Core

On paid plans, Secure Core routes your traffic through a server in a privacy-friendly country (such as Switzerland or Iceland) before it exits elsewhere. If an exit server were ever compromised, it still wouldn't see your real IP address.

### NetShield ad and malware blocking

NetShield, on paid plans, blocks known malware domains, and at its higher setting also ads and trackers, at the DNS level. It works across every app on your phone while the VPN is on, not just in your browser.

### Stealth protocol

On networks that block VPNs, such as some workplaces, schools or countries, the Stealth protocol disguises VPN traffic as ordinary web traffic. You can choose it in the app's protocol settings.

### Kill switch and always-on

Android's built-in "Always-on VPN" and "Block connections without VPN" settings work with Proton VPN, so nothing leaks if the connection drops. Find them under **Settings → Network & internet → VPN**, then tap the gear next to Proton VPN.

## Proton VPN free vs paid plans

| | Free plan | Paid plans |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ads | None | None |
| Data limit | None | None |
| Choose a country or server | No, Proton picks | Yes |
| Devices at once | 1 | Up to 10 |
| Secure Core and NetShield | No | Yes |

The free plan is genuinely usable every day for public Wi-Fi. Upgrade if you need a specific country, several devices, or the extra privacy features.

## How to install Proton VPN APK on Android

Install [Proton VPN from Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ch.protonvpn.android) if you can. Proton also publishes official builds outside Play, which is handy for phones without Google services.

1. Check your phone runs Android 8.0 or newer.
2. Choose an official source: Google Play, F-Droid, or the download link on [protonvpn.com](https://protonvpn.com/). Don't use other APK sites for a VPN.
3. If you downloaded the file from APKPeer, check that the SHA-256 matches the one on this page. We currently link to Proton's own sources, so there's no APKPeer file for Proton VPN.
4. Open the APK and allow your browser or file manager to **Install unknown apps** when Android asks.
5. Tap **Install**, then sign in or create a free Proton account.
6. Accept Android's VPN connection request, then turn off the install permission under **Settings → Apps → Special app access → Install unknown apps**.

## Is Proton VPN APK safe?

The official Proton VPN APK is safe, and it's one of the more trustworthy choices for a free VPN. Proton AG is a Swiss company, its Android app is open source, and its no-logs policy has been independently audited. Google Play doesn't mark it as containing ads, because it has none.

A few honest caveats. You need a Proton account, which means an email address. The free plan's limits are real: one device, and no choice of country or server. And no VPN makes you anonymous on its own; signing in to Google or Facebook still tells those services who you are.

The bigger risk with any VPN is a fake one. A modified VPN app can read everything you send. APKPeer only links to Proton's own downloads.
