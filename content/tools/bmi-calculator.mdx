## How to calculate your BMI

1. Choose **Metric** (centimetres and kilograms) or **Imperial** (feet, inches and pounds).
2. Enter your height and weight.
3. Your BMI, its category and the healthy weight range for your height appear instantly.

## The BMI formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

For example, someone 1.70 m tall weighing 68 kg has a BMI of 68 ÷ (1.70 × 1.70) = 23.5. In imperial units, the calculator converts pounds and inches to metric first.

## BMI categories for adults

| BMI | Category |
| --- | --- |
| Under 18.5 | Underweight |
| 18.5 to 24.9 | Healthy weight |
| 25 to 29.9 | Overweight |
| 30 or more | Obesity |

These are the World Health Organization's ranges for adults. Some health services, including the UK's NHS, recommend lower thresholds for people of South Asian, Chinese, Middle Eastern, Black African or African-Caribbean family background, because health risks start at a lower BMI.

## What BMI can and can't tell you

BMI is quick and needs only two measurements, which is why doctors use it for screening. But it has real limits:

- It **doesn't distinguish muscle from fat**, so muscular people can be classed as overweight.
- It **doesn't show where fat is stored**. Fat around the waist carries more health risk; waist measurement adds useful information.
- It **isn't for children and teenagers**, who are assessed using age- and sex-specific growth charts.
- It's **not suitable during pregnancy**.

Treat your BMI as a starting point. If you're concerned about your weight or health, a doctor or nurse can look at the full picture.
