## How to pick a color from an image

1. Tap **Choose an image** and select a photo or screenshot.
2. Tap or click anywhere on the image.
3. The color at that spot appears as a HEX code and RGB value, each with a copy button.
4. Below, the image's main colors are shown as a palette. Tap a swatch to copy its HEX code.

Large images are scaled down for display, but the colors are read from the image data, so the values are accurate.

## What the formats mean

| Format | Example | Use it in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HEX | #1E88E5 | Web design, CSS, design apps like Canva |
| RGB | rgb(30, 136, 229) | Code and apps that mix colors |

To get the same color in HSL, or to check its contrast for text, paste the HEX code into the [color converter](/tools/color-converter).

## Picking accurately

Photos aren't made of flat colors. Lighting, shadows, camera noise and JPG compression mean neighbouring pixels can differ slightly, even on what looks like one solid color. For a reliable result:

- Pick from the **middle of a color area**, away from edges and highlights.
- Try **two or three spots** and choose the most representative value.
- Use **screenshots or original graphics** rather than photos when matching exact brand colors.

## How the palette is made

The palette groups similar colors in the image and lists the most common groups, each averaged into a single swatch. It's a quick way to see an image's overall color scheme, which is useful for building a matching design, choosing text colors for a thumbnail, or matching an outfit or room to a photo.

## Ideas for using it

- Matching your **brand colors** from a logo file.
- Building a design that matches a **photo or app screenshot**.
- Choosing a **background color** for a favicon or app icon (see the [favicon generator](/tools/favicon-generator)).

The image is processed in your browser and never uploaded.
