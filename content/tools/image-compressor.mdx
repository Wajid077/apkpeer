## How to compress an image

1. Tap **Choose images** or drag your files onto the box. JPG, PNG and WebP all work, and you can add several at once.
2. Choose **Save as**. "Same format" keeps JPG and WebP as they are, and saves PNG as WebP so it actually gets smaller.
3. Set the quality. 80% is a good starting point.
4. Tap **Compress** and compare the before and after sizes, then download the files you want.

If an image isn't small enough, compress it again at a lower quality, or resize it first with the [image resizer](/tools/image-resizer). Smaller dimensions often save far more space than lower quality.

## How much quality should you keep?

| Quality | Typical saving on a phone photo | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 90% | 30–50% | Images you'll print or edit |
| 80% | 50–70% | Websites, email, most uploads |
| 60–70% | 70–85% | Strict upload limits, previews |

These are rough guides. Photos with lots of fine detail compress less; simple images with plain areas compress more. The tool shows the exact result for each file, so there's no guessing.

## Why compress images?

- **Upload limits.** Many forms cap files at 1–2 MB, while phone photos are often 3–8 MB.
- **Faster websites.** Smaller images make pages load faster, which visitors and search engines both notice.
- **Email and messaging.** Smaller attachments send faster and fit within size limits.
- **Storage.** Compressing large photo collections frees space on your phone or cloud storage.

## Privacy bonus: location data removed

Photos from phones usually carry hidden metadata, including the camera model and often the GPS location where the photo was taken. Because this tool re-encodes the image, that metadata is removed from the compressed copy. That's worth knowing before you post photos publicly.

## Nothing is uploaded

Everything happens in your browser. Your photos never leave your device, there's no sign-up and no watermark. For format changes rather than size, use the [image converter](/tools/image-converter).
