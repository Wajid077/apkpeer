## How to use the percentage calculator

There are three calculators on this page. Fill in the one that matches your question; results update as you type.

1. **What is X% of Y?** For example, 15% of 200 = 30.
2. **X is what percent of Y?** For example, 30 is 25% of 120.
3. **Percentage change from X to Y.** For example, from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase.

## The formulas

| Question | Formula | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| X% of Y | Y × X ÷ 100 | 200 × 15 ÷ 100 = 30 |
| X as a % of Y | X ÷ Y × 100 | 30 ÷ 120 × 100 = 25% |
| % change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 | (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = 25% |

## Common mistakes

**Percentage points vs percent.** If an interest rate rises from 4% to 5%, that's a rise of 1 percentage point, but a 25% increase in the rate itself. News reports often mix these up.

**Reversing a percentage change.** A price that falls 20% needs to rise 25%, not 20%, to get back to where it started. That's because the rise is calculated on the smaller, reduced price.

**Adding percentage discounts.** 20% off followed by another 10% off is 28% off in total, not 30%. The [discount calculator](/tools/discount-calculator) handles stacked discounts for you.

## Everyday uses

- **Exam marks:** 42 out of 60 is 70%.
- **Shopping:** what you save with a percentage discount.
- **Salaries:** the percentage increase of a pay rise.
- **Business:** growth in sales, followers or app downloads month to month.
- **Taxes:** how much GST or VAT is in a price (or use the [sales tax calculator](/tools/sales-tax-calculator)).

## Private and instant

All calculations happen in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent anywhere or saved.
