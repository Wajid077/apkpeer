## How to convert Roman numerals

1. Type a number from 1 to 3999, such as `2026`, or a Roman numeral, such as `MMXXVI`.
2. The converted value appears instantly.
3. Copy the result.

The converter checks that Roman numerals are written in the standard form and tells you if they're not.

## The seven symbols

| Symbol | Value |
| --- | --- |
| I | 1 |
| V | 5 |
| X | 10 |
| L | 50 |
| C | 100 |
| D | 500 |
| M | 1000 |

## The rules

1. **Add** symbols from largest to smallest: VIII = 5 + 3 = 8.
2. **Subtract** when a smaller symbol comes before a larger one: IV = 4, IX = 9, XL = 40, XC = 90, CD = 400, CM = 900.
3. Only I, X and C are used for subtraction, and only before the next two larger symbols.
4. A symbol is not repeated more than three times in a row.

Examples: 14 is XIV, 49 is XLIX, 99 is XCIX, and 1994 is MCMXCIV.

## IIII on clocks

You may see 4 written as IIII on clock faces. That's a long-standing clock-making tradition, possibly for visual balance with VIII on the other side, but it isn't the standard form, so this converter flags IIII and suggests IV.

## Where Roman numerals are used

- **Years** in film credits, on buildings and on graduation certificates.
- **Monarchs and popes**, such as Elizabeth II or Charles III.
- **Sporting events**, like the Super Bowl.
- **Book chapters and outlines**, and page numbers in prefaces.
- **Tattoos** of important dates, where getting the numeral right really matters.
