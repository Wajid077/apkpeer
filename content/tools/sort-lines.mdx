## How to sort lines

1. Paste your list into the **Input** box, one item per line.
2. Choose an order: **A → Z**, **Z → A**, **By length** or **Shuffle**.
3. Tick **Remove empty lines** if your list has blank lines in it.
4. Tap **Copy result**.

## Sorting options explained

| Order | What it does | Example use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A → Z | Alphabetical, numbers in natural order | Name lists, glossaries |
| Z → A | Reverse alphabetical | Ranking from the end |
| By length | Shortest lines first | Spotting very long or short entries |
| Shuffle | Random order | Quiz questions, draws, playlists |

The alphabetical sort uses **natural ordering**, which means numbers inside text are compared as numbers. "Episode 2" comes before "Episode 10", the way people expect, instead of the computer-style order that puts 10 first. It also ignores capital letters, so "android" and "Android" sort together, and it follows your browser's language rules for accented letters.

## Shuffling fairly

Shuffle uses a Fisher–Yates shuffle powered by your browser's cryptographically secure random number generator. Every possible order is equally likely, so it's fair for picking turns, randomizing questions or ordering a draw. If you need to pick winners from the list, the [random name picker](/tools/random-name-picker) does that directly.

## Tips

- Remove repeated entries first with [remove duplicate lines](/tools/remove-duplicate-lines), then sort.
- To flip a list into its reverse order without sorting, use [reverse text](/tools/reverse-text) and choose Line order.
- Use **Use result as input** to apply another step, such as sorting by length after sorting alphabetically.

## Private and instant

Sorting happens in your browser, so your list is never uploaded. It works quickly even for lists with tens of thousands of lines.
