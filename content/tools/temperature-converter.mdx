## How to convert temperature

1. Type a temperature in the **Value** box. Negative numbers work too.
2. Choose its scale: Celsius, Fahrenheit or Kelvin.
3. The other two scales update instantly.

## The formulas

| From → to | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Celsius → Fahrenheit | °F = °C × 9 ÷ 5 + 32 |
| Fahrenheit → Celsius | °C = (°F − 32) × 5 ÷ 9 |
| Celsius → Kelvin | K = °C + 273.15 |
| Kelvin → Celsius | °C = K − 273.15 |

A quick way to estimate Celsius to Fahrenheit in your head: double it and add 30. That gives 25 °C ≈ 80 °F (the exact answer is 77 °F), close enough for the weather.

## Reference points

| Description | °C | °F | K |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Absolute zero | −273.15 | −459.67 | 0 |
| Celsius and Fahrenheit meet | −40 | −40 | 233.15 |
| Water freezes | 0 | 32 | 273.15 |
| Comfortable room | 21 | 69.8 | 294.15 |
| Normal body temperature | about 37 | about 98.6 | about 310 |
| Water boils (sea level) | 100 | 212 | 373.15 |
| Moderate oven | 180 | 356 | 453.15 |

## Which countries use which scale?

Almost every country uses Celsius for weather and everyday life. The United States and a few others mainly use Fahrenheit, which is why recipes, weather apps and thermostats from the US often need converting. Kelvin is the scientific scale, used in physics and chemistry; it has no negative values and is written without the degree symbol.

## Phone temperatures

Android phones start to throttle performance and may show warnings when the battery gets hot, typically somewhere above 40–45 °C (104–113 °F), though the exact limits depend on the manufacturer. If a phone feels hot while gaming, letting it cool down protects the battery.
