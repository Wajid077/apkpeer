## How to use the word counter

Type or paste your text into the box. The counts update as you type:

- **Words**: groups of characters separated by spaces or new lines.
- **Characters**: every letter, number, symbol and space.
- **Characters (no spaces)**: the same, without spaces and line breaks.
- **Sentences**: text ending with a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark.
- **Paragraphs**: blocks of text separated by an empty line.
- **Reading and speaking time**: estimates based on typical speeds.

Tap **Clear** to start again. Nothing is saved, so copy your text elsewhere before closing the page if you need it.

## Common length limits

Many places limit how much you can write. Here are typical limits worth knowing:

| Where | Common limit |
| --- | --- |
| SEO page title | About 50–60 characters |
| Meta description | About 140–160 characters |
| Text message (SMS) | 160 characters per message |
| College application essay | Often 500–650 words |
| Google Play app description | 4,000 characters |
| Google Play short description | 80 characters |

Limits change between platforms and over time, so check the current rule for anything important. When a limit is in characters, check whether spaces count; this tool shows both numbers.

## How reading and speaking time are estimated

Reading time assumes about **200 words per minute**, a typical speed for reading on screen. Speaking time assumes about **130 words per minute**, a comfortable pace for presentations, speeches and voice-overs. Real speeds vary from person to person, so treat these as guides, especially for technical text, which most people read more slowly.

## Writing tips

- Aim for sentences of 15–20 words on average. Mixing short and long sentences keeps text easy to read.
- Keep paragraphs to three or four sentences, especially for reading on a phone.
- If you're over a word limit, cut filler words like "very", "really" and "in order to" before cutting ideas.

## Your text stays private

Counting happens in your browser as you type. Your text is never uploaded, stored or used for anything, so it's safe to paste drafts, essays or confidential documents.
